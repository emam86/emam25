A 5-star Nile cruise is the classic way to see Upper Egypt: you unpack once, the ship moves between Luxor and Aswan, and the big temples come to you. But "5-star" on the Nile covers a wide range. Two ships with the same rating can feel very different in cabin size, food, service and how crowded the sun deck gets.

This guide explains what the rating actually means, how the three common tiers compare, what is and is not included, which route to choose and when to travel. What "5-star" means on a Nile cruise

Nile cruise ships are licensed and classified by Egypt's tourism authorities, and most ships that sell to international travellers carry a 5-star classification. That classification sets a floor: air-conditioned en-suite cabins with river windows, a restaurant serving all meals, a sun deck with a pool, and a licensed guide programme.

What it does not do is tell you where a ship sits above that floor. That is why operators add their own labels: standard, deluxe and super deluxe (sometimes "luxury"). These are commercial terms, not official grades, so always ask what a label means for that specific ship: cabin size, year of last renovation, number of cabins, and guide-to-guest ratio.

Standard vs deluxe vs super deluxe: how the tiers compare

Our honest advice: for most first-time travellers a good deluxe ship is the sweet spot. The temples are identical whichever ship you choose, and the jump from standard to deluxe usually buys more comfort per dollar than the jump from deluxe to super deluxe.

What is included, and what usually is not

Package details vary, so always read the inclusions on the tour page. On our own cruises the following is typical:

Usually included

  • Cabin accommodation for the number of nights booked
  • Full board on the ship: breakfast, lunch and dinner
  • Guided sightseeing at the main temples on the route, with an Egyptologist guide
  • Entrance fees to the sites listed in the itinerary
  • Airport or railway-station transfers in Luxor and Aswan
  • Evening entertainment on board, such as a galabeya party or folklore show

Usually extra

  • Drinks with meals (soft drinks, alcohol, bottled water beyond what is provided)
  • Tips for the crew and guide
  • Optional excursions: Abu Simbel, the Luxor hot-air balloon, the Philae sound and light show
  • Entry into special tombs, such as Tutankhamun or Nefertari, that need a separate ticket
  • Flights or trains between Cairo and Luxor or Aswan, unless your package includes them
  • Your Egypt visa

Which route should you choose?

Almost every 5-star cruise sails the stretch between Luxor and Aswan, about 200 km of river with stops at Esna, Edfu and Kom Ombo. The real choice is direction and length.

When to go

  • October to April is the main season. Days are warm rather than hot, which matters because most sightseeing happens outdoors. December and January are the busiest months, so book early for the ship and cabin you want.
  • May to September is very hot in Luxor and especially in Aswan. Ships run, prices are often lower and sites are quieter, but plan sightseeing for early morning and late afternoon.
  • Holidays such as Christmas, New Year and Easter fill up fastest and often carry supplements.

How to choose the right ship

Before you book any 5-star Nile cruise, ask these questions. A good operator will answer them without hesitation:

  1. When was the ship built, and when was it last renovated?
  2. How many cabins does it have, and how large are they in square metres?
  3. Which deck will my cabin be on, and does it have a window, a French balcony or a real balcony?
  4. How large are the guiding groups, and are guides qualified Egyptologists?
  5. Exactly which sites and entrance fees are included?
  6. Are drinks, tips and transfers included or extra?

Frequently asked questions

Is a 5-star Nile cruise worth it?

For most visitors, yes. It combines transport, hotel, meals and guided sightseeing between Luxor and Aswan in one booking, and sailing past villages and farmland is part of the experience. If you dislike fixed schedules or groups, look at a private dahabiya or a land-based private tour instead.

How many nights do I need?

Three nights (Aswan to Luxor) covers the essentials. Four nights (Luxor to Aswan) gives the same sites with more time to relax on board. Add a night in Luxor before or after if you want to see the West Bank tombs at a slower pace.

What is the difference between a deluxe and a super deluxe Nile cruise?

Mostly space, food and service: larger cabins (often with balconies), more à la carte dining and smaller guiding groups. The sites you visit are the same. The labels are set by operators, not by an official body, so compare actual cabin sizes and inclusions.
